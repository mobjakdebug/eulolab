# Ground evidence plan — fins, sensors and bench (no flight approval)

**No measurements, board IDs, motor tests, fin inspections or instrument records from the user's hardware have been supplied.** Do not enter invented values into the product or interpret a compiled image, CSV header, manufacturer motor curve or USB ACK as a physical test. This checklist is for an identified rocket/electronics setup, qualified supervision and the applicable club/range/authority procedures; it is not a structural, electrical or launch-safety certification.

## Keep four kinds of information separate

| Item | Provenance in EuloLab | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dimensions, materials, airframe Cd, CP*, wind, damping, deployment | Editable **design/assumption** fields; initial values are illustrative, not measured on any user rocket | They can drive a conceptual model. A visually rendered fin is not an inspected fin. CP* and density defaults are not measured. |
| Klima C6/D9 thrust curve | Published motor **source curve** (`assets/motors/README.md`) | Not a static firing or proof of the user's motor, mount or installation. |
| Mass/CG, flight path, altitude, Mach, pressure q and apogee in Flight | **Predicted by an open-loop model** from the above assumptions | Not flight data or a launch decision. Estimated CG needs a real balance/scale cross-check. |
| Ground CSV sensor readings | **User-imported file**; a real recorder can create one via `tools/capture_serial.py` with a serial-port/hash trail | App cannot authenticate the device or sensor. Raw pressure/gyro/accel/temperature come from the supplied file; barometric altitude is derived relative to an observed stationary pressure. No GPS, absolute position, live PS4 sensor link or sensor-to-servo coupling. |

## Fin and mass inspection, *before* any powered rig

1. Record identity: body/fin drawing revision, actual materials, fin count, timestamp, operator, instrument model/serial and calibration status, units, ambient conditions. Photograph the fixture and datum; keep raw readings outside Git. A project dimension is a **nominal design value**, not proof that the built component matches.
2. Using a caliper, ruler and a repeatable reference datum along the tube, record each numbered fin's **root/tip chord, span, thickness, leading-edge sweep, fore/aft attachment position, radial spacing and cant/parallelism to the body axis**. Repeat measurements on each fin; note instrument resolution and uncertainty. Use a straight edge/angle fixture rather than judging the 3D preview. Inspect fillets, root attachment and tip stiffness/looseness with the propulsion system **disconnected**. Document deviations from the drawing; do not invent a universal acceptable angle or stiffness limit. The project's fin fields do not currently consume measured fin inspection data.
3. Weigh the **actual** airframe dry and in the relevant flight-ready configuration, and locate the balance point (CG) relative to a documented tail/nose datum. Record motor *type* and whether it is an inert mass substitute, recovery packing and electronics; obey local motor-handling procedures. Do not connect an igniter to a bench calibration setup. The program's mass-model CG is an estimate; compare with the measured CG and investigate differences rather than replacing the measured value with the estimate.
4. Obtain a documented **CP estimate** using a suitable method/tool for the actual geometry, and record its method, assumptions and uncertainty. EuloLab's CP* field is only a scenario input and its solver is not a validated Barrowman, structural or CFD calculation. Do not treat an assumed CP and modeled CG as a demonstrated stability margin. The [NAR safety-officer guidance](https://www.nar.org/content.aspx?page_id=22&club_id=114127&module_id=673715) specifically calls for fin attachment/alignment inspection and a flight-ready CG and substantiated CP. [NASA Glenn's stability overview](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/VirtualAero/BottleRocket/airplane/rktstab.html) describes CG/CP and a carefully conducted swing check; that is a separate supervised stability check, **not** a substitute for review of the whole vehicle or a launch permit.

## Sensors — table tests with a reference instrument

1. Identify exact MCU, I²C modules and addresses, serial connection and power rails. Set `hardware_config.h` **only for those parts**. Verify each library probe on the real board; disconnect each module and confirm the sketch emits an error and **stops**, not zeros or synthetic readings. Check reset/reconnect and missing serial lines. Use the exact data-unit header in `firmware/arduino/EuloRecorder/README.md`.
2. Secure the sensor board and record at least two **stationary** seconds at 20 Hz before motion. The Ground importer requires ≥10 readings spanning ≥1 s in the first two seconds, a gyro mean/residual within the software's 3 deg/s check, acceleration magnitude near 1 g and stable pressure. This is a parser quality gate, **not a sensor calibration certificate**. Record temperature and observed pressure zero in the report; no 1013.25 hPa sea-level constant is silently substituted.
3. Move the board by a known measured vertical distance on a **ground test rig** (no powered flight) and compare the derived *relative* barometric height and pressure change with an independent reference. Check repeatability and temperature/drift; venting or dynamic pressure can distort readings on a moving airframe. Rotate by a known marked angle about each **documented sensor body axis**; compare recorded gyro integral with a protractor/angle reference and quantify drift. Acceleration is logged but is not integrated into a GPS-free position estimate. Set acceptance tolerances **from your instruments/application**, not from an arbitrary software badge.
4. Preserve unaltered CSV and adjacent `.meta.json` from the capture tool, board/firmware/core/library versions, wiring, instrument observations and test operator. The app's file import is offline. It never independently attests the source of a CSV, and a timestamp/hash does not prove measurements were taken. **Do not** place contrived rows in a released-app sample-data folder.

## LoRa and servo are different tests

- Radio is opt-in **SX127x point-to-point status**, not LoRaWAN, a mesh or a high-rate flight telemetry recorder. Keep a local serial log to compare against what the receiver actually forwards; check both physical radios, antennas, frequencies, packet sequence/gaps, CRC failures, RSSI/SNR, supply, measured airtime and applicable regional limits. No radio connection is available in the PS4 app. Do not trust unencrypted/unauthed received bytes for actuation or safety decisions. UK users should review the specific [Ofcom IR 2030](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/spectrum/interface-requirements/ir-2030.pdf) sub-band and equipment conditions; those rules cannot be inferred from `868 MHz` alone.
- The separate EuloBench/2 USB bench needs **an identified board and valid FF/45/55 vendor bulk descriptors**, GPIO/timer wiring and a *physical* enable gate. With a motor/igniter disconnected and a mechanically safe unloaded linkage, first measure 50 Hz timing, neutral and ±2° **commanded pulse widths** at the output pin with a scope/logic analyser. Measure actual fin/servo travel separately using an angle reference or potentiometer; a USB ACK is only command receipt. Test disable gate, disconnect, power-loss reset, deadline, cooldown and independent servo-power cutoff. Do not attach a powered actuator based on a software-only port test. STM32 and Arduino ESP32 USB descriptor differences are documented in the firmware README and `docs/bench-usb.md`.

**Required to advance from compile/test to a validated hardware claim:** exact MCU/variant and revisions; sensor/radio and servo part numbers; reviewed schematic and power protection; observed USB descriptors; calibrated time/angle/voltage/pressure instruments; timestamped raw ground runs and photographs; tests of fail-closed and fault paths; and independent range/flight-safety review. No such evidence exists in this repository yet.
